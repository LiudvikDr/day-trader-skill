How the maths works
  • The room is a polygon; its area comes from the shoelace formula.
  • Each pattern is an infinite tiling (rows, or a lattice of motifs). It is rotated and shifted into the room, and every piece is clipped against the room outline with Sutherland–Hodgman polygon clipping.
  • A piece is full if its clipped area equals its own area, otherwise it is a cut. A cut narrower than ¼ of the piece in either direction is flagged as a sliver — hard to cut and likely to crack or look untidy.
  • Minimise cuts searches shifts across one repeat of the pattern and keeps the layout with the fewest slivers, then the fewest cuts.
  • Order quantity: tiles = every piece that touches the floor (cut tiles rarely reuse offcuts) plus 5 % spare. Boards = floor area ÷ board area plus the pattern's typical waste (7 % random stagger up to 15 % herringbone/chevron), because row-end offcuts start the next row.
Plans are estimates — always measure twice and confirm quantities with your supplier.
